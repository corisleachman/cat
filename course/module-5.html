Module 5 · about an hour

Putting it into practice

This week you will take stock of how far you have come, notice what is helping, and start preparing for life after the course.

Start here

Before you begin · two minutes

Settle first

Find somewhere you won't be interrupted. Put both feet on the floor and let the chair hold your weight. Breathe in gently through your nose for a count of four, and out slowly through your mouth for a count of six. Do this five or six times. Then name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can feel, two you can smell and one you can taste.

We start every module like this on purpose. A calmer body makes it easier to look at your thoughts with curiosity rather than alarm.

Recap

What did you notice?

Look back at last week's records. How aware were you of your thoughts, feelings and behaviour? Compare that to how it felt in Module 1, when you were just starting to spot your automatic thoughts. Have you been challenging your negative thoughts? Which tools have you found yourself reaching for?

Progress check

How far have you come?

Now is a good time to fill in the questionnaires from Module 1 again, and to compare your scores. Look at your 1 to 10 check-ins too.

Progress is rarely a straight line. There will be good weeks and harder ones. Look at the overall direction, and at the small changes: catching a thought sooner, believing it a little less, settling a little faster.

Do you need more support?

Be honest with yourself here. If your scores haven't shifted, have gone up, or if things feel heavier than when you started, that is not a failure. It is useful information, and it may mean you would benefit from more support than a self-guided course can give.

  • Your GP can talk through options with you.
  • In England you can refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies for free.
  • You are very welcome to get in touch with me about one-to-one counselling.

Preparing to finish

Looking ahead

Next week is the last module. Before then, take a few minutes to think about:

  • Which tool has been most helpful? Which do you want to keep using?
  • What situations are likely to be difficult in the coming months?
  • What early warning signs tell you things are starting to slip? (Sleep changing, withdrawing, a familiar thought coming back.)

Write your answers down. You will use them to build your plan next week.

This week

Your practice for the week

Homework

Record a real moment

This week, record an actual situation where you feel you applied what you have learned about challenging irrational thoughts. Write down what happened, the thought that came up, what you did with it, and how things turned out. Use a thought record if it helps.

It doesn't need to be a big moment or a perfect one. A small win, noticed, is worth a lot.

Look at how much you have shown up for yourself over these weeks. Whatever your scores say, that effort deserves to be recognised.
