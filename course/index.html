Why this course

Some thoughts arrive so fast and feel so true that they quietly run the day. They shape what you do, what you avoid, and how you feel in your body.

This course is a gentle, structured way to slow that down. Over six weeks you will learn the same practical Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) tools I use with clients, laid out step by step so you can work through them on your own. Each module takes about an hour, with a small practice to carry into your week.

It is also a space to pause. To give yourself time, to get to know yourself a little better, and to practise being kinder to yourself along the way.

You cannot always choose your first thought. You can learn to choose what happens next.

What you will come away with

Six weeks from now, you will have

  • I
    Confidence using CBT tools on your own

    You will know how to catch an unhelpful thought, check it against the facts, and find a more balanced one, so the thoughts and habits that get in the way of daily life, or of what you want to achieve, lose some of their grip.

  • II
    A better relationship with your feelings

    Rather than being swept along by emotions, you will be able to notice them, name them, and understand what they are telling you.

  • III
    An understanding of what your body is doing

    The racing heart, tight chest or churning stomach will make more sense. You will know why they happen and what helps them settle.

  • IV
    Ways to feel safer and more regulated

    Simple grounding and breathing practices that help bring you out of fight-or-flight and back into a calmer state, wherever you are.

  • V
    More self-awareness, and more self-compassion

    Time set aside each week to slow down, reflect, and practise speaking to yourself the way you would speak to someone you care about.

A little of what you will learn

Your body, your nervous system, and your thoughts

Fight-or-flight

An old alarm in a modern world

When your brain senses danger, it sounds an alarm. Adrenaline and cortisol are released, your heart beats faster, your breathing quickens and your muscles tense, ready to fight or run. Digestion slows, which is why anxiety can feel like butterflies or nausea.

That alarm kept our ancestors alive. The trouble is it cannot tell the difference between a predator and a difficult email, a worry about money or a critical thought. When it goes off often, or stays on, we feel on edge, exhausted and stuck. Understanding it is the first step to calming it.

The vagus nerve

Your body’s built-in brake

If fight-or-flight is the accelerator, the parasympathetic “rest and digest” system is the brake. The vagus nerve is a big part of it: a long nerve running from the brainstem down through the throat, heart, lungs and gut, carrying messages both ways between body and brain.

You can gently press that brake yourself. A long, slow out-breath, humming, splashing cool water on your face and feeling your feet on the ground all send a message of safety, so your heart rate settles and your thinking brain comes back online.

Grounding

Coming back to the here and now

Anxious minds tend to live in the future and low moods in the past. Grounding brings your attention back to this moment, through your senses: what you can see, hear, touch and feel right now. It interrupts the spiral and reminds your nervous system that, in this moment, you are safe enough.

Then, CBT

Why we settle first

It is hard to question a thought while your body is in alarm mode. So each module begins by settling, and only then turns to the CBT tools: noticing thoughts, checking the evidence, testing predictions and changing what you do. Calm body, clearer mind.

What is involved

Six modules, one a week

Is it right for you

Who this course is for

This course suits adults who are coping with everyday anxiety, worry, stress or low mood, and who would like practical tools they can use on their own. It works best if you can set aside about an hour a week, plus a few minutes most days for the practice.

It is not a replacement for therapy, and it is not designed for a mental health crisis. If you are struggling a lot right now, please speak to your GP, or get in touch with me about one-to-one counselling. The course can also sit well alongside it.
