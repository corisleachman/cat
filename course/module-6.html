Start here

Before you begin · two minutes

Settle first

Find somewhere you won't be interrupted. Put both feet on the floor and let the chair hold your weight. Breathe in gently through your nose for a count of four, and out slowly through your mouth for a count of six. Do this five or six times. Then name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can feel, two you can smell and one you can taste.

We start every module like this on purpose. A calmer body makes it easier to look at your thoughts with curiosity rather than alarm.

Your toolkit

The tools you now have

These are yours to keep. They help you stay in charge of your own thoughts, actions and behaviour.

  • Settle
    Calm the body first

    Long out-breaths, grounding through your senses, and the vagus nerve brake. Use them before you try to think things through.

  • Notice
    Spot the pattern

    The hot cross bun. Notice, capture, write it down. Name the feeling.

  • Check
    Fact-check the thought

    Rational or irrational? Put a camera on it. Evidence for and against. Find the balanced thought.

  • Pause
    STOP before you react

    Stop, take a step back, observe, proceed mindfully. Remember that feelings are not facts.

  • Test
    Try it out

    Behavioural experiments and graded steps to test predictions instead of avoiding them.

  • Act
    Do what nourishes you

    Plan activities that bring achievement, closeness and enjoyment, especially when your mood is low.

Looking back

How far you have come

Use the 1 to 10 scale at the top of this page one more time, and compare it to your very first check-in. If you filled in the resilience questionnaire in Module 1, try it again now and see which areas have grown.

Reflection · 15 minutes

A letter to yourself

Write a short letter to yourself, to read on a hard day in the future. Include what you have learned, which tools help most, the early warning signs to watch for, and what you would say to yourself, kindly, when things feel difficult. Keep it somewhere you will find it.

Keeping it going

Support for the road ahead

The skills you have practised get stronger with use, like a muscle. Keep challenging your negative thoughts and keep a mood journal, even when things are going well. That is when the practice is easiest, and it makes the tools easier to reach when you need them.

Useful websites

  • NHS Every Mind Matters: free, practical tips and a personalised mind plan.
  • Mind: clear information on mental health and where to get help.
  • Get Self Help: a large library of free CBT worksheets and information.
  • NHS Talking Therapies: free NHS therapy you can refer yourself to (England).
  • Samaritans: someone to talk to, any time, on 116 123.

And if you would like ongoing, one-to-one support to build on this work, I would be glad to hear from you. Make an enquiry.

Your practice for the week

Take away

Keep going, gently

  • Keep challenging your negative thoughts.
  • Keep a mood journal.
  • Start each day, or each hard moment, by settling your body first.
  • Come back to these modules whenever you need a refresher.

You have given yourself six weeks of time, attention and care. Keep offering yourself that kindness. You deserve it.
