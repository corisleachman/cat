01 Where the work begins

You are holding it together. Most days, it works. But coping, managing and getting through are not the same as feeling well.

02 How things feel now

When getting through the day takes everything

You are functioning. You get up, you do what needs doing, you keep other people going.

But the effort it takes has quietly gone up. Sleep is thinner. Patience is shorter. Things that used to feel manageable now feel like a lot.

None of that means something is wrong with you. Usually it means something has been carried alone for too long.

03 The first conversation

You do not need to arrive with the right words.

You only need to start talking, at your own pace, with someone whose job is to listen carefully and stay alongside you.

You do not need to be in crisis to deserve support, and you do not need to have it worked out beforehand.

05 Two kinds of support

Counselling & career coaching

Some people come to me for counselling, some for career coaching, and some move between the two at different stages.

Counselling

A space to explore how you feel

Confidential, at a pace that suits you. My work is integrative and trauma-informed, drawing on person-centred, CBT-informed and solution-focused approaches — adapted to you rather than fitted to a rigid model.

It can help with anxiety and low mood, stress and burnout, grief and bereavement, confidence and self-esteem, life transitions, and neurodivergent identity — whether long known or newly being explored.

Career coaching

Structured, practical, forward-facing

For navigating a change of direction, a loss of confidence at work, a return after illness, or questions about purpose and next steps.

It is psychologically informed, because of my background in mental health — but it is not therapy, and it does not involve the treatment of mental health conditions.

06 What to expect

How I work

Confidentialityand care

What you bring to sessions stays between us. I keep brief, secure notes in line with professional and legal requirements, and your information is not shared without your consent — except in the rare circumstances where there is a serious concern about safety. A full privacy notice is available on request.

Collaborationnot instruction

We decide together how the work goes. There is no fixed number of sessions, and we review things together as we go. I adapt the approach to you rather than applying one model to everybody.

Ethical practiceand supervision

I work in line with the BACP Ethical Framework and under regular clinical supervision, where I reflect on my practice to make sure it stays safe and ethical. Clients are never identifiable. I work within the limits of my competence, and if another form of support would serve you better, I will say so and help with signposting.

09 No waiting list, no pressure

How it works

Getting started is deliberately simple. Nothing is decided before you are ready.

  1. You get in touch

    Send an enquiry using the form below. I read every message myself and aim to respond within five working days.

  2. A short initial conversation

    A brief, free conversation to explore what you are looking for and whether I am the right person to support you. There is no obligation to continue beyond this.

  3. Your first session

    Fifty minutes, online by secure video. We start wherever feels manageable — you do not need to have prepared anything.

  4. We agree how to work

    Counselling is usually weekly or fortnightly. Career coaching can be more flexible. We agree what suits you and review it together as we go.

  5. We continue, at your pace

    There is no fixed number of sessions. Some people come for short-term support around one issue; others prefer longer-term work.
